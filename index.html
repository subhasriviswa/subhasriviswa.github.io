About

I am a PhD candidate in Neuroscience at the Université de Montréal (CHU Sainte-Justine Azrieli Research Centre), supervised by Dr. Patricia Conrod. My research focuses on characterising normative trajectories of adolescent brain connectivity development and how cannabis and alcohol perturb these trajectories — using sign-aware graph-theoretical metrics and within/between-person decomposition to formally dissociate predisposition from consequence.

My background bridges clinical psychology (B.Sc. Psychology, Women's Christian College; M.Sc. Clinical Psychology, CHRIST University, Bangalore) and computational neuroimaging. Before my doctorate, I worked as a Project Associate at the Centre for Addiction Medicine, NIMHANS Bangalore, leading a large-scale population study on tobacco use among 600,000+ COVID-19 patients.

Research Projects
Doctoral project — Adolescent brain network development & substance use (NeuroVenture cohort)
Using longitudinal fMRI (~155 adolescents, 3 timepoints, ages 12–18), I characterise normative trajectories of functional connectivity with sign-aware graph-theoretical metrics and formally dissociate intra- from inter-individual effects of cannabis and alcohol using hierarchical mixed models (Mundlak decomposition). Task-based fMRI (Stop-Signal, MID) extends findings to cognitive control and reward processing.
